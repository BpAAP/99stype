Overview & History

The Jaguar S-Type name carries a rich history. The modern S-Type (X200) was built at Castle Bromwich, Birmingham, from 1999 to 2008, reviving a badge first used in the 1960s.

The Original S-Type (1963–1968)

The first Jaguar S-Type was a mid-size saloon derived from the Mark 2, produced from 1963 until 1968. It used the Mark 2 body with a restyled boot, independent rear suspension, and inboard rear brakes — hence the “S” for sports.

The Modern S-Type (X200)

Launched for the 1999 model year, the X200 was Jaguar’s return to the mid-size executive market, rivalling the BMW 5 Series and Mercedes E-Class. Styled by Geoff Lawson and Ian Callum’s team, it consciously echoed the 1960s Mark 2 with its quad-headlamp face and rounded cabin.

It shared the Ford DEW98 platform with the Lincoln LS and Ford Thunderbird, using a steel monocoque, rear-wheel drive, and a fully independent suspension. The range covered V6 and V8 petrol engines, a twin-turbo V6 diesel, and the high-performance S-Type R.
